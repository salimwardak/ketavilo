Frankenstein

Mary Shelley

فارسی · پښتو · English

Victor Frankenstein creates a living being and then abandons responsibility for it. Seeking affection and a place in society, the creature repeatedly encounters rejection. Nested narrators complicate any simple division between an innocent creator and an entirely monstrous creation.

Key ideas

  • The ability to create something does not ensure readiness for its consequences.
  • Isolation and rejection matter deeply, although suffering does not justify violence.
  • Several narrative perspectives invite readers to reconsider responsibility and sympathy.

Something to consider

What responsibilities remain after we bring something new into the world?
